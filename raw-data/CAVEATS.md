# Caveats

A few things you should know before you dig in. I'm giving you raw data only,
on purpose — I want you to imagine this from zero, not inherit anyone's design.

The messages file contains other people's actual private messages. Jacob's
entire business is that people trust him. Use it for patterns — who talks to
whom, how often, how fast — but never store, quote, or surface what anyone
wrote. That's a hard line, not a preference.

The two files in voice-and-context are not clean. They're the only record we
have of Jacob talking about his business and his people, but a previous
analyst's ideas and framing are woven through them. Take what Jacob himself
says as gold. Treat everything around it as one team's take that you're free
to ignore.

The biggest trap in this data: Jacob's closest relationships barely show up in
it. His best people live on the phone and in person, so LinkedIn sees almost
nothing of them. If your model reads low activity as low value, it will bury
exactly the people he'd name first. Absence of evidence here is absence of
evidence, nothing more.

The roster is his hand-made working list and it's incomplete in a telling way —
some people he clearly relies on aren't in it, and the people he's closest to
are sometimes entered with only a first name. The gaps and the sloppiness are
signal, not noise.

And this identifies real school-district officials by name, with Jacob's candid
notes about them. Keep it private. Don't publish it, don't commit it anywhere
public, don't let it leave the project.
